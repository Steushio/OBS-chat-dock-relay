OBS Studio Integration Guide

STEP-BY-STEP SETUP FOR CROSS-LINUX BROADCASTERS

⚙️

Why are settings unified?

To deliver a premium User Experience (UX), all active parameters, port selections, and resource modes have been integrated into the main Cockpit Dashboard. This prevents you from switching back and forth, allowing you to configure, test, and preview everything in a single, high-performance panel.

How to Add Custom Docks in OBS

1 Open OBS Studio Docks Menu

In OBS Studio, navigate to the top toolbar, click on Docks, and select Custom Browser Docks....

2 Register the Local URLs

Copy the URLs from your Cockpit Dashboard and paste them into the OBS Docks window:

  • Add Dock Name: YouTube Chat & URL: http://127.0.0.1:3000/chatdock
  • Add Dock Name: YouTube Alerts & URL: http://127.0.0.1:3000/alertdock
  • Add Dock Name: System Log & URL: http://127.0.0.1:3000/eventdock
3 Apply and Position

Click Apply. The custom docks will float on your screen. You can drag and snap them directly into the OBS sidebar layout next to your video feed! They will persist automatically next time you open OBS.

💡

Pro-Tips for Linux Broadcasters

  • Zero-Latency Switch: The local websocket serves events in real-time. When a stream transitions, the OBS Docks never need to be reloaded. They remain open and automatically start pulling chats from the new stream!
  • Low Resource Mode: Running on single-board computers or older GPU hardware? Check the Low Resource Mode box in settings. It disables downloading author profile pictures, bringing overlays to near-zero CPU and memory usage!
  • Start Minimized: With Run on Startup enabled, the app launches in the system tray, scanning silently in the background without stealing visual focus.
